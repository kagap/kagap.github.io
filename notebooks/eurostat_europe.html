Jobs and prices across Europe, in plain SQL¶

Monthly unemployment (2005 onwards, total and under-25) and consumer price inflation (2015 onwards, for all items, food, housing and energy, and transport) for about 40 European countries from Eurostat. Eight questions in SQL only: ranking countries with RANK, change against a year earlier with LAG(..., 12), peaks and the months since with ROW_NUMBER, a pivot by conditional aggregation, a correlation coefficient built from sums, and a gaps-and-islands query for the longest runs of falling unemployment. Python only runs the queries and draws the charts.

Data: the Eurostat dissemination API (une_rt_m and prc_hicp_manr), free to reuse with attribution. Run python build_db.py once to download the series (about 3 MB) into a SQLite database, then point EUROSTAT_DB at that file.

Reading the data. Unemployment is the seasonally adjusted share of the labour force. Inflation is the annual rate of change of the harmonised index (HICP). Countries report with different lags, so a "latest month" differs a little between them, and some series stop early (the United Kingdom's unemployment ends in September 2020). The EU and euro-area aggregates are used as benchmarks and left out of the country rankings.

1. Which countries have the highest unemployment right now?¶

ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY country ORDER BY month DESC) finds each country's latest month, and LAG(rate, 12) inside the same CTE gives the value a year earlier. RANK() then orders the countries. Only countries with data from 2026 are shown.

In [3]:
latest = q("""
WITH series AS (
    SELECT country_code, month, rate_total, rate_youth,
           LAG(rate_total, 12) OVER (PARTITION BY country_code ORDER BY month)  AS year_ago,
           ROW_NUMBER()        OVER (PARTITION BY country_code ORDER BY month DESC) AS rn
    FROM unemployment
)
SELECT RANK() OVER (ORDER BY s.rate_total DESC)  AS rank,
       c.country,
       s.month,
       s.rate_total                              AS unemployment_pct,
       ROUND(s.rate_total - s.year_ago, 1)       AS change_vs_year_ago,
       s.rate_youth                              AS under25_pct
FROM series s
JOIN countries c USING (country_code)
WHERE s.rn = 1 AND c.is_aggregate = 0 AND s.month >= '2026-01'
ORDER BY s.rate_total DESC
""")

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(8.5, 7))
colors = [BLUE if v < 0 else AMBER for v in latest.change_vs_year_ago.fillna(0)]
ax.barh(latest.country[::-1], latest.unemployment_pct[::-1], color=colors[::-1])
for y, v in enumerate(latest.unemployment_pct[::-1]):
    ax.text(v + 0.1, y, f"{v:.1f}%", va="center", fontsize=7)
ax.set_title("Unemployment rate, latest month (amber = higher than a year earlier, blue = lower)")
ax.tick_params(axis="y", labelsize=8)
fig.tight_layout()
plt.show()

2. Is youth unemployment a different problem?¶

The same latest-month CTE, now comparing the under-25 rate with the overall rate. The ratio says how many times likelier a young active person is to be out of work than the average.

In [4]:
youth = q("""
WITH series AS (
    SELECT country_code, month, rate_total, rate_youth,
           ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY country_code ORDER BY month DESC) AS rn
    FROM unemployment
    WHERE rate_total IS NOT NULL AND rate_youth IS NOT NULL
)
SELECT c.country, s.month,
       s.rate_total                          AS overall_pct,
       s.rate_youth                          AS under25_pct,
       ROUND(s.rate_youth / s.rate_total, 1) AS youth_to_overall_ratio
FROM series s
JOIN countries c USING (country_code)
WHERE s.rn = 1 AND c.is_aggregate = 0 AND s.month >= '2026-01'
ORDER BY under25_pct DESC
LIMIT 12
""")

3. How bad did the crisis years get, and who recovered?¶

Two ROW_NUMBER rankings per country (by unemployment, highest first, and by month, latest first) pick the peak and the most recent value in one pass, using conditional aggregation to put them on one row.

In [5]:
peaks = q("""
WITH ranked AS (
    SELECT country_code, month, rate_total,
           ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY country_code ORDER BY rate_total DESC, month DESC) AS peak_rn,
           ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY country_code ORDER BY month DESC)                  AS last_rn
    FROM unemployment
    WHERE rate_total IS NOT NULL
)
SELECT c.country,
       MAX(CASE WHEN peak_rn = 1 THEN rate_total END)              AS peak_pct,
       MAX(CASE WHEN peak_rn = 1 THEN month END)                   AS peak_month,
       MAX(CASE WHEN last_rn = 1 THEN rate_total END)              AS latest_pct,
       MAX(CASE WHEN last_rn = 1 THEN month END)                   AS latest_month,
       ROUND(MAX(CASE WHEN peak_rn = 1 THEN rate_total END)
           - MAX(CASE WHEN last_rn = 1 THEN rate_total END), 1)    AS fall_since_peak
FROM ranked r
JOIN countries c USING (country_code)
WHERE c.is_aggregate = 0
GROUP BY r.country_code
HAVING latest_month >= '2026-01'
ORDER BY peak_pct DESC
LIMIT 12
""")

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(9, 5))
y = np.arange(len(peaks))
ax.barh(y + 0.2, peaks.peak_pct, height=0.4, color=AMBER, label="Peak")
ax.barh(y - 0.2, peaks.latest_pct, height=0.4, color=BLUE, label="Latest")
ax.set_yticks(y, [f"{c} ({m[:4]})" for c, m in zip(peaks.country, peaks.peak_month)])
ax.invert_yaxis()
ax.set_xlabel("Unemployment rate (%)")
ax.set_title("Highest unemployment since 2005 (year of the peak) against today")
ax.legend(frameon=False)
fig.tight_layout()
plt.show()

4. How has Poland compared with the EU average?¶

Conditional aggregation pivots Poland and the EU-27 average into two columns of one monthly table, so the gap between them is a simple subtraction.

In [6]:
poland = q("""
SELECT month,
       MAX(CASE WHEN country_code = 'PL'        THEN rate_total END) AS poland,
       MAX(CASE WHEN country_code = 'EU27_2020' THEN rate_total END) AS eu27,
       ROUND(MAX(CASE WHEN country_code = 'PL'        THEN rate_total END)
           - MAX(CASE WHEN country_code = 'EU27_2020' THEN rate_total END), 1) AS gap
FROM unemployment
WHERE country_code IN ('PL', 'EU27_2020')
GROUP BY month
HAVING poland IS NOT NULL AND eu27 IS NOT NULL
ORDER BY month
""")

5. Where did the 2022 price shock hit hardest?¶

ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY country ORDER BY annual_rate DESC) finds each country's peak year-on-year inflation (all items) and the month it happened.

inflation_peak = q("""
WITH ranked AS (
    SELECT country_code, month, annual_rate,

7. Do countries with higher unemployment have lower inflation?¶

Yearly averages per country are joined, and the correlation coefficient across countries is computed for each year from sums (n*Σxy - Σx*Σy over the square roots of the variances). A negative value would match the textbook trade-off between unemployment and inflation.

In [9]:
phillips = q("""
WITH u AS (
    SELECT country_code, substr(month, 1, 4) AS year, AVG(rate_total) AS unemployment
    FROM unemployment WHERE rate_total IS NOT NULL
    GROUP BY country_code, year
),
i AS (
    SELECT country_code, substr(month, 1, 4) AS year, AVG(annual_rate) AS inflation
    FROM inflation WHERE coicop = 'CP00'
    GROUP BY country_code, year
),
joined AS (
    SELECT u.year, u.unemployment AS x, i.inflation AS y
    FROM u JOIN i USING (country_code, year)
    JOIN countries c USING (country_code)
    WHERE c.is_aggregate = 0 AND u.year BETWEEN '2015' AND '2025'
),
sums AS (
    SELECT year, COUNT(*) AS n, SUM(x) AS sx, SUM(y) AS sy, SUM(x * y) AS sxy, SUM(x * x) AS sxx, SUM(y * y) AS syy
    FROM joined GROUP BY year
)
SELECT year, n AS countries,
       ROUND((n * sxy - sx * sy) / (sqrt(n * sxx - sx * sx) * sqrt(n * syy - sy * sy)), 2) AS correlation
FROM sums
ORDER BY year
""")

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(9, 3.8))
ax.bar(phillips.year, phillips.correlation, color=[BLUE if v < 0 else AMBER for v in phillips.correlation])
for x, v in enumerate(phillips.correlation):
    ax.text(x, v + (0.02 if v >= 0 else -0.06), f"{v:.2f}", ha="center", fontsize=8)
ax.axhline(0, color="#4a5568", lw=0.8)
ax.set_ylim(-1, 1)
ax.set_title("Correlation between unemployment and inflation across countries, by year")
fig.tight_layout()
plt.show()

8. Which country had the longest run of falling unemployment?¶

A gaps-and-islands query. LAG flags every month in which unemployment did not rise, and the difference between a country's overall month number and its month number within the flag stays constant for as long as a run lasts, so grouping by it collapses each run into one row.

In [10]:
streaks = q("""
WITH changes AS (
    SELECT country_code, month, rate_total,
           rate_total - LAG(rate_total) OVER (PARTITION BY country_code ORDER BY month) AS delta
    FROM unemployment
    WHERE rate_total IS NOT NULL
),
flagged AS (
    SELECT *, delta <= 0 AS not_rising,
           ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY country_code ORDER BY month) AS month_no
    FROM changes
    WHERE delta IS NOT NULL
),
numbered AS (
    SELECT *, ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY country_code, not_rising ORDER BY month) AS flag_no
    FROM flagged
)
SELECT c.country,
       COUNT(*)              AS months_in_a_row,
       MIN(n.month)          AS from_month,
       MAX(n.month)          AS to_month,
       ROUND(SUM(n.delta), 1) AS change_pp
FROM numbered n
JOIN countries c USING (country_code)
WHERE n.not_rising = 1 AND c.is_aggregate = 0
GROUP BY n.country_code, n.month_no - n.flag_no
ORDER BY months_in_a_row DESC, from_month
LIMIT 8
""")

What the queries say¶

  • Unemployment is highest in the Balkans, Spain and Finland. In the latest month, Bosnia and Herzegovina is at 11.4%, North Macedonia 11.0%, Spain 10.0% and Finland 9.9%. At the other end are Czechia (3.2%), Slovenia, Malta and Poland (3.4% each), and Japan (2.5%) as a non-European benchmark.
  • Youth unemployment is a different multiple of the problem. Romania's under-25 rate is 30.8%, 4.5 times its overall rate of 6.8%. Estonia is at 28.4% (3.5 times), and Spain, Finland and Sweden are above 22%.
  • The crisis peaks were enormous, and recoveries long. Greece peaked at 28.3% in July 2013 and is now at 7.4%; Spain peaked at 26.4% in February 2013 and is still at 10.0%; Latvia went from 20.7% (January 2010) to 7.3%. North Macedonia's peak of 36.7% came earlier, in August 2006. Poland started the series at 18.1% in 2005, peaked at 18.4% that June, and is at 3.4% now.
  • Poland crossed below the EU average in 2008 and stayed there. In January 2005 it was 8.3 points above the EU-27 (18.1% against 9.8%). It has been below the EU average in nearly every month since June 2008, and the gap now is 3.4% against 6.1%.
  • The 2022 price shock was far bigger in the east and the Baltics. Türkiye peaked at 85.4% (October 2022). Within the EU, Hungary reached 26.2% (January 2023), Estonia 25.2% (August 2022), Lithuania 22.5% and Latvia 22.0%. Poland peaked at 17.2% in February 2023.
  • Energy led, food followed. For the EU as a whole, housing and energy inflation peaked first and highest at 23.2% (October 2022), transport peaked at 16.0% (June 2022), and food peaked later at 19.2% (March 2023). The headline rate topped out at 11.5% in October 2022. Transport prices fell below zero in the spring of 2020.
  • There is no stable trade-off between unemployment and inflation across countries. The yearly correlation between the two ranges from -0.16 (2015) to +0.28 (2022), close to zero and slightly positive in the most recent years, which is the opposite sign from the textbook curve.
  • Long recoveries are visible in the streaks. Spain's unemployment did not rise for 69 months (August 2013 to April 2019) and fell 12.0 points. Croatia managed 61 months (-11.1 points), Poland 42 months from 2005 (-11.8 points). Germany's 81-month run (February 2013 to October 2019) removed only 2.2 points because it started from a low level.

Limits: countries report with different lags, so "latest" is a different month for each (all in 2026); the UK series stops in 2020; the unemployment definitions follow Eurostat's harmonised method, so they can differ from national figures; averages across countries treat a small economy and a large one equally.

Techniques covered: ROW_NUMBER and RANK per group, LAG with an offset of 12, conditional-aggregation pivots, a correlation coefficient from sums, CTE chains and a gaps-and-islands query.
